# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is AI Netscape?

AI Netscape is a 1997-era HTML editor with one impossible button: a modern AI that builds a complete, state-of-the-art website from a one-line brief, in under a minute. No signup, no payment, modem sounds included.

It is a love letter to the early web, executed with 2026 tools.

## Is this affiliated with the original Netscape?

No. The original Netscape browser was discontinued in 2008. The trademarks are currently held by Verizon Communications through its acquisition of AOL. AI Netscape uses the Netscape name in a parody and homage capacity, with no claim of affiliation, endorsement, or trademark interest.

If you worked on Netscape Composer in 1997, we owe you something.

## Who built this?

A solo developer in West Vancouver, British Columbia, with help from AI coding tools and substantially more cups of coffee than the original Netscape Composer team probably had per capita.

## How does the AI Composer work?

You type a brief description of the website you want. The AI Composer sends your brief to a large language model (currently xAI's Grok as primary engine, with Anthropic's Claude as fallback), receives a complete HTML page in return, and renders it inside the editor. The full process takes 15-90 seconds depending on complexity and modem conditions.

The page is yours to keep. You can edit it, save it, share it, or generate a new one.

## Why is the modem sound so loud?

It is calibrated to the actual volume of a 1997 USRobotics 56k modem connecting at 28.8 kbps. Some compromises were made for the safety of your speakers, but not many. You may upgrade to 56k via the button in the status bar; this does not reduce the volume.

## Why does it take so long?

The AI Composer runs over a simulated 28.8 kbps connection.

(In actuality the connection is significantly faster than that. The wait time is the AI thinking. We could remove the modem sound and make the wait feel shorter, but we won't.)

## Can I make my page private?

Yes. When you share a generated page, the share dialog includes a "Make site public" checkbox. Uncheck it to keep the page accessible only by direct URL, hidden from the Gallery.

You can change this setting later by visiting your page (using the same browser that generated it) and toggling the public/private control that appears in the bottom-right corner.

## What happens to my page if I close the browser?

The page is saved to our servers at the moment of generation. As long as you remember the URL, you can return to it indefinitely. If you remember the URL, congratulations — you have one of the most reliable forms of digital memory.

We do not currently offer an account system or a "my pages" dashboard. This is a feature for a later version.

## Will you add user accounts?

Probably. The architecture is designed to support adding accounts later, and we have placeholder fields ready for that day. We have not committed to a timeline.

When accounts arrive, your existing pages will be claimable via the author cookie that was set when you created them. If you clear cookies, you lose this connection. Please act accordingly.

## Can I delete a page I generated?

Yes. Email webmaster@ainetscape.com with the URL and the author cookie token. We will verify and delete.

If you have lost the author cookie, contact us and we will use reasonable judgment.

## Does the "Cool" button do anything?

It records that you found this page interesting. The vote count is visible on the Gallery and on the page itself. Cool votes contribute to the weekly Site of the Week selection.

You can vote once per page. Trying to vote twice will be politely ignored.

## Why is the date always 1997 in the Gallery?

To maintain the conceit. AI Netscape has been running since 1997. Some elements are aware of this.

(The pages were of course generated recently. We log accurate timestamps internally for our own records. The displayed "May 24, 1997" is a stylistic choice.)

## Why a 1997 aesthetic?

Several reasons. The 1997 web was a moment when the medium was young enough to feel like anyone could participate. The chrome of that era — the chunky bezels, the table layouts, the `<font>` tags — represents an aesthetic confidence that has not really existed since. AI Netscape is in part an argument that the modern web has lost something by becoming so smooth.

(Also it makes a funny joke.)

## How is the AI Composer different from ChatGPT or Claude?

The AI Composer uses many of the same underlying models. The difference is that the output is wrapped in a specific frame — a 1997 web editor — and constrained to produce a single self-contained HTML page. The constraint is part of the point.

You could ask ChatGPT or Claude for the same kind of page and get a similar result. AI Netscape gives you the experience of doing so inside a Netscape Composer that didn't exist in 1997, but should have.

## Can I trust the generated content?

The AI may produce errors, invent facts, or generate text that resembles existing copyrighted material. Treat AI-generated content as a starting point, not a finished product. Verify any facts before publishing. Check that you have the right to use anything that looks too professional.

## What does "best viewed in 800x600 with 256 colors or better" mean?

Nothing. The Service works on any modern browser. The statement is a 1997 reference, included for atmospheric purposes.

## Is there a mobile version?

The Service works on mobile devices at reduced fidelity. The full 800-pixel chrome is best viewed on a desktop browser. We did not have mobile phones in 1997.

## I have a problem with a page someone else generated.

Use the "Report" link on any page. Reports go to webmaster@ainetscape.com. We review reports manually and remove content that violates our Terms of Service.

## I want to report a bug.

Email webmaster@ainetscape.com. Include the page URL if relevant, the browser you are using, and a description of what went wrong. Screenshots help.

## Will there be more features?

Possibly. The road map includes:

- A user account system for claiming and managing your generated pages
- More AI engines in the waterfall, including Google Gemini
- An expanded set of brief templates beyond the four currently shown
- Tools to remix and modify generated pages with follow-up prompts

We have not committed to dates for any of this. AI Netscape is a project rather than a roadmap.

## Why are you doing this?

Several reasons, including but not limited to:

- The early web was good and we miss it
- AI is going to change how websites are made and we wanted to be there at the start
- It is funny
- The first place we got paid to make websites was Netscape Composer in 1997, and there is a sentimental loop to closing
